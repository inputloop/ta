## The five that only you can make

Cutting to the useful signal, since that was the ask. These are the slides where my
drafting adds nothing and your presence is the content:

- **#9** — the settled guess table after the weekend. I can build the before; the after
  is the proof, and it does not exist until you run it.
- **#10** — your calibration curve. Nobody else's calibration is interesting.
- **#33/34** — a real metric from a real workplace, failed live. The risk in that demo is
  the reason it lands, and only you can decide whose metric to use.
- **#57** — the David story. Provenance of a method from a person, told by the person it
  was taught to. This is the slide that makes the method not-generic.
- **#64** — "who it lets in." That is your dim6 question, in your voice, and it is the
  only possible closing slide.

## Three recommended paths

- **Ten minutes (a lightning talk):** 1 → 2 → 4 → 23 → 27 → 29 → 35 → 41 → 42 → 64.
  The loop, the trap, the refusal, the surprise, the close.
- **Forty minutes (a workshop):** add tracks 2 and 5, and run #34 live with a volunteer's
  metric. Budget half the time for the argument that follows — it is the good part.
- **The video:** 1–7 as the spine, #12 (the graph paper) as the opener so it starts in
  your hand and not in a template, #41–42 as the worked example, #57 for the human
  origin, #64 to close.

## The name — settled

**CALLED SHOT.** Blessed by Corwin 2026-08-28 ("Called shot is rad, let's ride that.
Bless."). You call the shot before you take it, then settle the difference. It names the
load-bearing move, and it covers the risk canon in the same breath — pricing a risk is
calling a shot about the future, and the bearing ratio is calling one about yourself.

**LENS stays his word** for the four-point figure. Rejected alternates kept as contrast:
`Delta-Check` (closest to the page's DELTA-√; plainer, less voice) and `The Waist` (a good
name for the figure, but the figure already has one).

Retitle the deck accordingly: *"Called Shot — a method for saying what you expect before
you find out."*
