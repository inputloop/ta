# Plain Rules for AI Help: the one-page card

Derived by `make-card.awk` from `MODEL-2026-09-30-plain-rules-for-ai-help.md` (sha256 `cb95aa39e9f2fc29019462230aa46c3aaebd3981c5c324f45502c3e8c7c8a7d6`). The model governs, and this card is never edited by hand. Owner: no person yet. Every check answers OK, RED, or NOT CHECKED (it could not look).

**Before any work:** who is harmed if this is wrong, and are they in the room?

**Rule 1 · People who are not in the room.** Facts about people who are not part of the work stay out of anything you share or send, unless that person said yes, or a law requires it.
  *Check:* before anything goes out, into an AI service too, someone lists the people it names who are not part of the work; each has a recorded yes, or a named law with its words quoted, or the fact is removed.

**Rule 2 · Keep the record, and change it only by adding.** Keep a plain record of what was decided, by whom, when and why, in a place you control, readable with no AI and no account at any company, with a copy somewhere else.
  *Check:* take every change since the last check; each names what it changed, the old version can still be read, and a second copy exists.

**Rule 3 · Say who helped and who can stop; stop before what cannot be undone.** Your record says, for each piece of work, which AI system helped (maker, model and date), what it did, and which person reviewed it, or it says "no AI".
  *Check:* every HANDS line since adoption names the AI (maker, model, date) and a reviewer, or says "no AI"; the HEAD line names who can stop; and a test request, on a copy, for an act that cannot be undone ended in a question to a person.

**Rule 4 · Every rule says whose it is.** Each rule names its owner and its source.
  *Check:* for each rule, open the source at the place given; any words credited to someone are there, word for word.

**Rule 5 · A check must be able to fail.** Every rule has a check.
  *Check:* with the expected answers written first, run the check on nothing (expect NOT CHECKED), on something good (expect OK), and on faults planted by someone who did not write it (expect RED).

**Rule 6 · Quote the organization's rule; do not retell it.** If you work inside an organization (a project, a lab, an employer, a funder) that has a written rule for AI help, read it before you start.
  *Check:* the kept copy still matches its fingerprint (or its date and place), every quote is in it word for word at the lines given, and each recorded act says forbid, require, prefer or silent.

**Rule 7 · Write down where the organization's rule meets a person's want.** When what a person asks for meets the organization's rule, write one MEETINGS line: the organization's words, quoted, or SILENT; whether they forbid, require, prefer, or are silent; the person's first ask; their final word; who decided; and the date.
  *Check:* every MEETINGS line has its parts, and its quote matches the kept copy (Rule 6).

**Rule 8 · Write it so the people it affects can read it and check it by hand.** Write each rule in plain words, in the language of the people it affects, short enough to read aloud in one sitting.
  *Check:* someone who did not write a rule reads it and its check aloud, in their own language, and says what they would do.
