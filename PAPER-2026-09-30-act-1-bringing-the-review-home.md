## Top copy — the composite

*(Written last, after §1–§8; placed first.)*

**What the work is.** It is a way to write down the rules an AI assistant works under so that three things stay
true:
- each rule says **whose it is**;
- each claim carries **the check that could prove it wrong**;
- where an organization's written rule and a person's instruction meet, the meeting is **recorded, not guessed**.

It comes in three layers: a home model (`operating-model-BASE.md` 1.0.51 and an org model), a shipped kit (t0 v1.0),
and a review that abstracted eleven subjects from both, plus a lab (C4, polygram and the publication shelves) where
the practice is worked out.

**Where it stands on 2026-09-30.** On runs, not readings, it stands as follows.

1. **Its sharpest result is a finding about itself.** Lines the instrument drafted were installed in his file,
   blessed and re-signed by him as a file, and later cited back as his own rules. Both archaeology arms settle part
   00 L18 and L20 as the instrument's, from its own install log. He assented to the file, not authorship of the
   line. A detector built blind from the review's "owner on every rule" design separated the two on a sealed test.
   v0.1 caught 6 of 9 AI-written rules, cleared 9 of 9 of his, and raised no false flags. v0.2 catches 7 of 9, but
   that re-score is not blind (§5a item 2). The shipped finder caught 2 and false-flagged 4.
2. **When a second hand applies the method from a written spec, in a second language, it catches what the shipped
   tools miss, and fails closed so hard that it is not yet usable.** On sealed consent records, the rebuilt gate
   printed 0 false passes where the shipped gate printed 12 of 19. It also passed 0 of the 6 good records. On the
   one privacy set neither side wrote (another arm's 12 sealed plants), the rebuilt scanner flagged 7 and the
   shipped one refused 6, so the gap there is one plant. It never printed a value.
3. **Its own tools mostly break its first rule.** Two independent censuses, in two languages, agree on 46 of 47 exit
   codes. Of the 47 tools both readers ran, the same 29 exit 0 over empty input (P1A counts 30 of its 48, P1B 29).
   Not all of these print a pass word; some make no exit promise. One tool exits 77, "not checked".
4. **Its central effect claim, C1, is not shown.** Two blind graders found attribution adds nothing beyond
   specificity (0 of 5 tasks, 5 ties). Both specific arms sat at the ceiling, so the honest word is "not shown"
   rather than "refuted".
5. **Its multi-party side has met an organization's written rule, but not yet a disagreement it can count.** GNU
   Emacs' own written AI rule (`AGENTS.md`) is on record. Its text and commit were later attested by a permitted fetch
   and the keeper's own clone (§5a item 9). The record credits that rule with a scope that came from a person (§5a item 4). The census finds 8 of 56
   decisions decided by an org's written rule:
   - 5 by outside orgs, two of them one rule acting twice;
   - 3 by the keeper's own org, one of whose "rules" is an unblessed proposal the instrument wrote.
   "None against a person" depends on which turn is counted, and the census does not record which (§5a item 12).
6. **The home carries the contradictions the review charged only to the kit.** The review never opened the home.
   Both independent layer readers found the same six primary contradictions with the OFFERING (P4A lists 8 rows in
   all, P4B 7). Two of the six are in the home models: a widened stop rule under the keeper's name, and an
   org-over-person rank. The other four are in the kit and t0. One home line ranks tools ahead of the canon's first
   value.

**Measured against today's AI governance** (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act with its 2026 amendment,
OECD), two independent crosswalks used different categories. The mapper found 20 of 122 asks covered, 76 partly
and 26 not. The skeptic found 25 of 99 that it can produce, 43 in prose only, and 31 not at all. It is strongest on
checks that can fail, human oversight, and the provenance of its own instructions. The mapper names impact on
people, capacity and third parties as weakest; the skeptic adds a door for outsiders.

**Measured against its own canon's first value** ("Universalism and egalitarianism first", CANON.md L15), its front door is narrow: shell
only, English only, no glossary, and adopters pointed only at proprietary AI services.

**The composite.** This is a rigorous self-audit of one person's shop by one model family. Its bearing on the wider
world is real in two places:
- **instruction provenance:** whose rule is this, and can the record show it;
- **checks that can say "no" and "not checked".**

It is not yet evidence about organizations, other people, or effect. Its best opportunities put exactly those in
the room:
- a second party who can disagree ("put your organization into the room");
- a reader outside the model family;
- a front door that reaches people without a shell or English.

Its floor (§2) is nine capabilities. Five are already shown to work on copies, one is shown in part, and three are
not yet shown (corrected in §5a).

## Glossary: the house's words, in plain words

- **Called shot.** Write down what you expect before you try, then compare. The difference is the product.
- **Canon.** The keeper's signed statement of his values (`CANON.md`, `PRINCIPLES.md`).
- **Door / part.** A rule file an AI assistant loads at the start, built from small rule pieces ("parts").
- **Floor.** The smallest set of things that must always hold.
- **Green when blind.** A check that reports success when it looked at nothing.
- **HIS / MINE.** Whether a rule's words are the keeper's own, or the AI assistant's.
- **Instrument.** The AI assistant, as the shop calls it.
- **LENS.** The keeper's way of holding several views apart before handing work back.
- **NOT CHECKED (exit 77).** The honest third answer when a check could not look.
- **Org rung.** The place in the order of precedence where an organization's written rule sits.
- **Persona.** A named character or role-voice an AI agent is set up to speak as.
- **Prove-red.** Deliberately planting a fault to show the check catches it.
- **Seal.** The fingerprint (sha256) of a file recorded before anyone could see what it predicts.
